What Does the Research Say?

Recent studies explore if CBD is able to improve sleep. In 2025, a clinical trial evaluated an oral CBD-terpene mixture’s effects on individuals facing insomnia.

  • Participants received 300 mg of CBD and terpenes – fragrant compounds present in cannabis besides other plants – nightly for four weeks.
  • The results indicated that, on average, subjects experienced somewhat longer periods in deep (slow-wave) sleep, as well as REM sleep compared to those administered a placebo, about 1.3% more each night overall.

That small increase, which may not seem like much, translated to a substantial improvement for some individuals. Those who initially had poor sleep gained as much as 48 extra minutes of restful sleep each night over the month. Importantly, participants reported no adverse effects.

CBD Alone: Is It Enough?

Here’s an interesting point: big improvements were not seen by all participants. The benefits were most apparent in people who had lower quantities of deep, as well as REM sleep at the beginning. It is also important to keep in mind that the research used only CBD and terpenes, leaving out THC. You will not experience the sensation of being stoned, so you won’t have to stress about being groggy the subsequent day.

Other investigations present conflicting findings regarding regular CBD (excluding THC, in addition to other cannabinoids). Some investigations suggest that combining CBD together with modest amounts of THC is more powerful when managing certain types of sleeplessness than using one alone.

What Do the Experts Say About CBD for Sleep?

Most experts consent that there’s encouraging proof to help CBD as an opportunity for improving certain aspects of sleep quality.

Particularly if you have problems with falling asleep in addition to staying asleep because of anxiousness or pain-related issues. It won’t magically solve all your sleep troubles overnight.
